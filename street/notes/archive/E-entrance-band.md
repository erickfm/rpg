# The entrance band: my answer is NO, do not carry the stripes across it

The desk asked directly: *"If the stripes should carry across it, that is a
route to D, not a defect in your field. Tell me if you think they should and I
will route it."*

**They should not. Route D a TONE change, not stripes.**

## What that band actually is

Between the gate and the loop you cross about 4 m of the site's ground —
`openSite` in `ct/street.ts`. In a municipal park that strip is **paving**: the
apron a gate opens onto, where people stop, where the noticeboard and the bin
stand. Every real park has one. It is not lawn that has been left unmown.

## So the fault is its TONE, not its pattern

The user's complaint about this surface has always been the same word — it
reads as **carriageway**. That is a colour and grain problem, and it is the same
one I fixed on my own loop path: `#7d7565` sat between the road's `#46413a` and
the walk's `#84817a` and collapsed onto the road in anything but flat noon
light. Buff hoggin at `#9c8b66` fixed it, and the desk confirmed independently
that it *"reads properly as a park path."*

The same move works here and nothing else needs to change: **warm it and lighten
it away from the carriageway.** Paving, not asphalt.

## Why striping it would be worse

1. **It would delete the forecourt.** A gate needs somewhere to arrive; running
   mown grass to the railings leaves the entrance with no threshold and puts
   turf under the bin and the noticeboard.
2. **Stripes are a MOWN-FIELD signal.** They say "this is the big open bit."
   Putting them on the 4 m strip you walk across says the field starts at the
   railings, which makes the actual field — the panel inside the loop — read as
   a second, smaller lawn rather than the main event.
3. **It moves a seam rather than fixing one.** My striped field ends at the
   loop because the loop is its edge. Extending stripes across another module's
   ground means two modules drawing one continuous pattern and agreeing about
   its phase for ever.

## What I would route

→ **D**: the park's site ground reads as carriageway from the gate, which is
the canonical station. Warm and lighten it so it reads as paving. No stripes,
no geometry change, no seam with my field — it stops at the loop either way.

Measured for reference, since it is the whole of the complaint: the loop path is
`#9c8b66`; the carriageway is `#46413a`.
