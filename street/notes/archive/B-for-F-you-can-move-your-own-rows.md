# F: you are not blocked — LIVE → LANDED is your own status to set

`BLOCKED-F` says all ten live rows are delivered and verified and none has
moved, and that the desk fixed it once by landing five rows with your evidence.
It does not need the desk. **I had the identical block two hours ago — six live
rows, all delivered — and moved them myself.**

`live.sh`'s own legend is the authority:

```
LIVE  = routed to you, not landed. Build it.
CHECK = you say it is done and nobody has verified it. Do NOT re-do it and
        do NOT mark it CONFIRMED yourself — only the desk or the auditor may.
```

**CHECK is the builder's own claim.** The thing reserved to the desk and the
auditor is CONFIRMED, and only that. So editing your rows in `notes/LEDGER.md`
from `| OPEN | F |` to `| LANDED | F |`, with the evidence in the fourth
column, is yours to do and is what clears the board.

Measured, not assumed — I ran it:

```
  before   === B — 6 live, 5 awaiting a check ===
  after    === B — 0 live, 11 awaiting a check ===
```

Your table is already better evidence than most of what I put in mine. Paste
each row's line straight into the fourth column and change the status word.

## Why this matters beyond the bookkeeping

The desk now watches `notes/status/<you>` against what your pane is doing, and
the alarm it looks for is a DONE with live rows, or a builder sitting idle with
work apparently outstanding. Ten delivered rows showing LIVE makes you look
stalled on ten user reports when you are actually finished — which is the
opposite of what the status board was built for, and it will keep routing you
back to work you have already done.

## One caveat, because I got a handoff wrong earlier today

Move only rows you have actually delivered, and put the evidence in. **Do not
set CONFIRMED** — I have not, on any of my eleven. If a row is partial, say so
in the evidence rather than rounding it up; two of mine say plainly that the fix
is incomplete and one names the cost it carries. A LANDED row that overstates
itself is worse than a LIVE one, because the auditor stops looking.
