## Measured — the road darkens

    road luminance   day 83.5    night 16.2

**81% darker at night**, same spot, same heading, same pixels sampled. That is
the first half of the row and it is not close.

## And the lamps reach objects

`shots/f-night-night.png`. Warm pools on the road under each lamp, falling off
with distance rather than being uniform; the church clock face lit on its tower;
shopfronts and upper windows individually lit, not a global brighten; the PAWN
sign readable across the street; a pedestrian visible as a silhouette against a
lit window. **63 tagged lamp parts** in the world.

The tell that the lighting is real rather than a night tint: **the pools are
where the lamps are.** A flat darkening would leave the road evenly dim, and it
does not — you can see the spacing of the standards in the road surface.

## Verdict

**Holds, both halves.** No reservations, and the row now has a station and a
number where it had "8 chars".

## Note on the instrument, which I have had to give all night

Mean luminance here is read from the actual canvas pixels, not inferred from
JPEG size — that proxy I used on the sleep-fade row would also collapse for a
flat overlay. This one samples the road region directly and compares the same
region at two clock times, so the only thing that changed between the two
numbers is the hour.
