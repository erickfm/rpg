## The stations, in O's own order

**1 — anywhere in the side street facing east.** Holds, and the strong form of
it holds: *"you should be able to tell what it is before you can read the
plate."* From 17 m out the rusticated stone base, the deep-set barred windows in
a plain brick upper and the heavy double doors read as a municipal lock-up
before `CITY OF CROSSTOWN / HOUSE OF DETENTION` is legible at all. It closes the
street cleanly — the pawn shop's LOANS fascia on one side and SEVENS' neon on
the other, and the jail dead ahead terminating the view.

**3 — at the door, press E.** Live at 0.21 m from where walking east stops you,
and E puts you inside.

**4 — walking up to the counter. IS THE SERGEANT LOOKING AT YOU OR PAST YOU.**
**At you.** Standing in front of the counter he is drawn in the atlas's FRONT
view, face to the room, behind the counter rather than beside it — and he is
still facing you from the far end of the cell corridor 12 m away, which is the
harder case and the one the 8-angle atlas earns its keep on. Not a plane.

**5 — through the gate, in the corridor with bars both sides.** Holds. Bars run
the full height both sides, cell doors set back behind them, the corridor
terminating on the counter so the sergeant is in frame from inside the block.

**6 — a cell through the bars, AND THEN A CELL ON THE OTHER SIDE.** This is the
one O flagged (*"the mirror is where the bug hides and it deserves the second
look"*), and it is **clean**. I looked at both runs from the corridor centreline
in one frame: the bar spacing, the cell-door setback, the green dado behind and
the light fittings are symmetric about the aisle, and neither run is a copy of
the other with its handedness left over. No §33/§41 fault visible from where a
player stands.
