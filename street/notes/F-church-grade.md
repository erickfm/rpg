# F — graded my own church nave, and named what is still weak

Not a live row. Queue empty, so I applied the user's own test to a room I own
and had only ever checked for doors and steps: *would this impress someone who
has been disappointed*.

`shots/f-church-nave.png`, standing just inside the street door.

## What works

The narthex arch frames the nave, and the eye travels altar → crucifix →
window, which is what the composition was built for. The **crucifix is clearly
there and centred** — the user's *"this church is catholic wheres the jesus on
the cross?"* is answered and reads from the entrance, not just up close. Pews
fill both sides, the aisle runs clean to the chancel, the stained glass sits
above the altar, and the stations of the cross march down both walls. The
coursed piers I fixed earlier hold up the frame either side.

## What does not, and I am not pretending otherwise

**The upper nave walls are large blank plaster.** Between the stations and the
ceiling there is a lot of flat pale nothing, and the ceiling is another. It is
the same fault class the user named in the bodega and I have now fixed three
times elsewhere — an untextured expanse with no joints to give it scale.

The honest fix is not more texture, it is **clerestory windows**: a nave that
tall has a row of high windows, they are what a church's upper wall IS, and
they would light the space from above instead of leaving it flat. That is a
real piece of work, not a tweak.

**I am not starting it with the context I have left.** Half-built windows I
cannot walk, shoot and grade would be worse than the blank wall, and this
session has already produced one nine-room false alarm from work done in a
hurry. Logging it as the next item on this room instead.

## Also worth knowing

The pews merge into two solid brown masses when seen from the entrance. Up
close they are correct — individual seats, backs, and 18 of them sittable —
but at nave length the silhouette flattens. Probably wants a lighter top rail
or a gap that reads at distance. Lower priority than the walls.
