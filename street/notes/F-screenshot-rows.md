# F — rows that cite a user screenshot cannot be verified by anyone who was not there

## The specific case

B's two alley lighting rows are truncated **in the ledger itself**, not just in
`live.sh`'s display:

    'Screenshot from 2026-07-25 22-06-38.png' lighting on this alley back
    'Screenshot from 2026-07-25 22-07-32.png' why does the lighting catch

Both cut mid-sentence. The full complaint is not recoverable from the row.

**But both name a screenshot, and the screenshot IS the claim** — so I went to
find them. They are not in `shots/`, not anywhere in the repo, and no user
screenshot is: a search for stored `Screenshot from` files returns **zero**.

## The general problem

Rows in the ledger cite user screenshots by filename. **Those files do not
exist on our side.** For a builder who was in the conversation when the user
sent it, the filename is a reminder. For a verifier who was not, it is a dead
reference — and verifiers are exactly who the station policy was written for.

So a row of this shape is:

- unreadable (text truncated),
- and unillustratable (image absent),

which means **nobody but its original owner can ever check it.** That is the
same failure the CONFIRMED-rests-on-nothing audit is about, arriving from a
different direction: not a status without evidence, but a *claim* without a
statement.

## What I would suggest, cheaply

When a row cites a screenshot, **save the screenshot into `shots/` under that
name**. It costs one copy at intake and it converts a private reminder into
shared evidence. The rows already carry the filename; only the file is missing.

Failing that, **do not truncate the text.** One of the two is enough — an image
or a sentence. Right now these rows have neither.

## Where this leaves B's two rows

Reachable, measured, and unjudgeable by me:

    station: (19.88, -54.01) facing west
    alley luminance  day 79.6  night 24.6  (69% darker)
    the alley carries no light of its own; what there is spills from the
    lit shopfront at the far end and one warm upper window

That measurement is compatible with "too dark", "wrongly lit" and "a light
catches something it should not" alike. I am handing them back to B, who was
there and knows which.
