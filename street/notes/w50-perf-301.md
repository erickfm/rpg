# w50 — item 128: performance drops in flat 301

**The user:** *"i get awful performance drops in my room not sure why."*

**HANDED BACK, NOT FIXED.** The desk's strong lead is **disproved**, and the real
cause is not yet named. What follows is what is now known, so the next builder
starts from measurement instead of from the same wrong premise. Port **4187**
(dev), proved free first; shut down at the end.

---

## The desk's lead is wrong — the guard it asks for already exists

The item says:

> `ct/apartment.ts:2683` sets `tvRedraw = 0.11`, so the television **repaints its
> entire canvas and re-uploads the texture roughly 9 times a second**, forever,
> whether or not anyone is looking at it.

**It does not.** Two guards sit *above* that line and both return early:

- `apartment.ts:2630` — `if (Math.abs(lastGy - 2 * ST) > 0.5) return;` — nothing
  happens unless the player is on the TV's own floor.
- `apartment.ts:2638` — `if (!tvLit) return;` — and `tvLit` is set at `:2632`
  **only when the player is seated at the TV seat** (within 0.20 m of
  `TV_SEAT_X`/`TV_SEAT_Z`). Standing up sets it false, paints the dead screen
  once, and stops.

So the repaint costs nothing while the player is merely standing in his room,
which is the situation he is describing. The item's own "candidate to rule out"
— *whether the TV keeps painting when the player is not in 301 or not facing it*
— is **already answered in the source: it does not.**

Confirmed at runtime, not just read: standing in 301, the world publishes
`tv: {seg:"crosstown price", on:false, warming:false}`. `on: false` is `tvLit`.

## The measurement, and why the obvious version of it lies

**Do not compare rAF frame times in headless.** My first probe did, and the same
room sampled twice gave **78.6 ms then 35.0 ms** — a 2x swing with nothing
changed, because headless Chromium is still warming up (JIT, shader compiles,
texture uploads) for the first several seconds. Every A/B built on that measures
the stopwatch. `scripts/probes/w50-perf-301.mjs` records the trap.

A V8 sampling profile after a 10 s warm-up (`scripts/probes/w50-profile-301.mjs`)
gives, over 10 s standing in 301:

| self time | what |
|---|---|
| **77.4%** | `(program)` — native. Headless renders on software GL, so rasterisation dominates and this number says nothing about the user's GPU |
| ~4.1% | **raycasting** — `raycast` 182 ms + `intersectsObject` 134 ms + `intersect` 96 ms |
| 1.3% | `needsUpdate` (texture upload) |
| rest | three.js matrix/render internals, individually under 1% |

Of the ~21% that is JS at all, **raycasting is the single largest identifiable
block** — roughly a fifth of all JS time — and it is running while the player
merely stands there. That is the lead I would chase next: what is raycasting every
frame in 301, and does it need to.

**The headline caveat: 77% native under software GL means this environment cannot
answer "how bad is it on his machine".** The JS ranking above is portable; the
frame-time figures are not. The item asks for the F readout before and after, and
that is the right instrument — it needs a real GPU, i.e. the user's own browser,
or a run on a machine with hardware GL.

## Not fixed, and what I would do next

1. **Name what raycasts every frame in 301** and whether it can be gated the way
   the TV already is. Most likely the `[E]` spot picker; `__ct.spots()` and
   `reachMargin()` are already published for exactly this kind of question.
2. **Get an F reading from the user, or from hardware GL.** Everything here is
   JS-side attribution; the drop he feels may be GPU-side and invisible to it.
3. **Leave the TV alone** — the repaint is already gated to a seated player, and
   "repaint only when the frame changes" would be optimising a path that does not
   run in the reported situation.
