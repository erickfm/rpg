# F verifying the bodega frontage — reads, but the glass is doing nothing

    station:   stand on the pavement at (3.4, -91.5) looking south-east
    predicate: red BODEGA sign band legible from down the street

## What works

The sign band is the strongest piece: big, red, legible from well down the
block, and it names the shop instantly. Brick above, a street tree, the
frontage sits properly in the run of the street.

## What does not: the windows are near-black and advertise nothing

The glazing along the frontage reads as a dark band with faint reflections and
**nothing behind it**. For most buildings that is fine. For this one it is the
single thing underselling the shop, for a specific reason:

**A corner shop's windows ARE its advertising.** Beer and lottery posters, a
neon OPEN, hand-lettered card signs, cigarette decals, an ice cooler sticker,
the stacked stock pressed up against the glass. That clutter is most of what
makes one recognisable from the street — more than the sign band, because you
see it from an angle and at a distance. Right now the frontage is a clean sign
over a dark void, which reads as *closed* or *vacant* more than as a shop.

This is the same standard the user has been applying inside: *"attention to
detail and decor, I want it to feel real"*, and for the alley, *"these should
be recognizable"*.

## Why I am confident it is worth doing

The interior behind that glass is now dense and legible — stocked shelves,
lit coolers, a lottery machine, a keeper. **None of it is visible from the
street**, and I am not asking for it to be: rooms live out in their slabs, so
the glass can never show the real interior. What it can carry is the
advertising layer, which is flat art on the glass and cheap.

So the ask for B is not "show the inside". It is: **put the posters on**.

## Not mine, and stated plainly

The frontage is B's. The interior behind it is mine, and I have no view on how
B builds this — only that from a player's eye the frontage currently promises
less than the room delivers, which is the wrong way round for a shop the user
has filed four separate complaints about.
